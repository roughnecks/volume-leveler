# Volume Leveler 🎶

**Volume Leveler** is a Windows application that automatically balances the volume of every app playing sound on your PC, so you never have to reach for the volume mixer again.

A screenshot (Windows 10) can be found in the "media" folder.

<img src="media/logo.png" width="250" alt="Volume Leveler Logo">

## Here’s how it works:

- Set your speaker or headphone volume once, at a comfortable level, and leave it there — Volume Leveler adjusts each app’s Windows volume, not your hardware volume
- Before starting, make sure Windows’ master volume and each app’s volume are at or near 100% — Volume Leveler can lower a loud app, but it needs headroom to raise a quiet one
- Press Start to begin tracking active apps
- Set your target loudness: either drag the reference slider, or play something in an app you consider “just right,” select it from the dropdown, and press “Seed reference from selected app”
- Give each app a few seconds of continuous playback — Volume Leveler automatically calibrates and settles it at your chosen level
- Use Ctrl+Alt+L anytime to pause leveling temporarily without closing the app
- Save up to three reference presets for different situations (e.g. dance night vs. background music) and switch between them with one click
- If an app should never be touched (like a notification sound), right-click it in the app list and add it to the blacklist
- Use the tray icon for quick access to everything: showing the window, help, link to this website, and options to start leveling and/or start with Windows automatically
- If something feels off, use “Reset volumes” to bring every tracked app back to a neutral starting point and recalibrate

## You’re set!

All of the music you play from that moment, onward, will stay leveled to the reference value you set – doesn’t matter how many sources your music comes from. But that’s not all! If you open another audio stream, maybe a YouTube video in your browser, forget about fixing its volume; Volume Leveler will adjust it for you, to the same reference value seen before.

The app works almost in real time; calibrations happen when a volume change is detected, but also on tracks change.. For this to work, and avoid false positives, calibrations take their time (seconds anyway) – that’s why it’s “almost” in real time.

At its current state, Volume Leveler is made to work with music; using it while watching a movie can result in a different/unwanted behaviour.
